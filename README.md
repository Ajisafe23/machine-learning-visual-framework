# machine-learning-visual-framework
Visual framework and research exploration of major machine learning algorithms, their classifications, and real-world applications.

## Overview
Machine Learning (ML) is a branch of Artificial Intelligence (AI) that allows systems to learn patterns from data and make decisions or predictions without being explicitly programmed for every task. Deep Learning (DL) is a subset of ML that uses layered neural networks to process complex patterns, especially in images, speech, and text. Generative AI focuses on creating new content such as text, images, audio, and code.

## Relationship Between AI, ML, DL, and Generative AI

Artificial Intelligence (AI)
•	Broad field focused on building systems that simulate human intelligence.
Machine Learning (ML)
•	A subset of AI where systems learn patterns from data.
Deep Learning (DL)
•	A subset of ML that uses neural networks with multiple layers.
Generative AI
•	A specialized branch of AI focused on generating new content such as text, images, audio, and videos.

Flow of Relationship:
AI → Machine Learning → Deep Learning → Generative AI

## Application Domains
Application Domains

Machine learning algorithms are applied across different domains depending on the type of data being analyzed and the problem being solved. Some algorithms perform better with structured numerical datasets, while others are specifically designed for images, text, audio, or content generation tasks.

**Tabular Data**

This domain involves structured datasets organized in rows and columns, such as spreadsheets and databases. Algorithms in this area are commonly used for prediction, classification, and trend analysis.

**Examples:**
Sales forecasting
Fraud detection
Customer analytics

**Computer Vision**
Computer Vision focuses on helping machines interpret and analyze images or video data. Deep learning algorithms are heavily used in this domain.

**Examples:**
Facial recognition
Medical image analysis
Autonomous vehicles

**Natural Language Processing (NLP)**
NLP focuses on understanding and processing human language in text or speech format.

**Examples:**
Chatbots
Language translation
Sentiment analysis

**Generative AI**
Generative AI focuses on creating new content such as text, images, audio, videos, or code using learned patterns from existing data.

**Examples:**
AI image generation
Text generation tools like ChatGPT
AI-assisted content creation

## Research Notes and Brainstorming

This framework represents part of my ongoing exploration into the relationship between Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, and Generative AI, especially within GIS, infrastructure analytics, and public sector operations. Rather than looking at these concepts only from a classroom or theoretical perspective, I wanted to better understand how different machine learning algorithms could realistically support operational systems, spatial analytics, and infrastructure decision-making.

Coming from a GIS and infrastructure management background, I naturally started connecting many of these algorithms to projects I currently work on within Department of Public Services. For example, while working on the Catch Basin Cleaning Inventory and Monitoring Application and the Winter Operations Tracking System, I started brainstorming how clustering algorithms such as K-Means could potentially help group areas with similar flooding trends, service request patterns, or maintenance activity levels.

Decision Trees and Random Forest algorithms also stood out to me because of how useful they may become for predictive infrastructure management. Looking at historical roadway conditions, maintenance records, weather trends, operational data, and asset performance could eventually help identify areas that may require attention before failures occur.

The deep learning side of this research was particularly interesting because it connects heavily with imagery, automation, and spatial intelligence. Computer Vision models like CNNs could eventually support automated bridge inspections, roadway damage detection, asset inventory extraction from drone imagery, or identifying infrastructure defects directly from field photographs collected in the field.

Another area I found interesting was the relationship between Generative AI and GIS workflows. As AI tools continue evolving, there is strong potential for integrating AI-assisted reporting, dashboard summarization, operational insights, and automated geospatial analysis into public infrastructure systems and enterprise GIS environments.

This framework also reflects my broader interest in building practical and scalable AI-supported GIS solutions rather than focusing strictly on theoretical machine learning concepts. Since GIS continues becoming increasingly data-driven, understanding how these algorithms connect to spatial analytics, automation, infrastructure management, and operational decision-making is becoming more important professionally.

Overall, putting this framework together helped me better organize my understanding of how different machine learning algorithms relate to various domains including tabular analytics, computer vision, natural language processing, and generative AI while also helping me connect them to practical GIS and infrastructure management applications that I currently work with professionally.
